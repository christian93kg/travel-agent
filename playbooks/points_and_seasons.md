# Points, Miles, and Shoulder Seasons

**What this framework claims:** Two reference tools, not an essay — check before booking, don't read end to end. Points and miles are treated here as an access tool for reaching a specific peak experience (per Bill Perkins' Die With Zero argument for spending down rather than hoarding), not a balance to accumulate. Shoulder-season windows are the calendar-level lever for getting most of a season's payoff — weather, conditions, availability — at a fraction of the peak-season cost and crowd.

## Points and miles — access, not savings

A points balance is a depreciating asset: award charts get worse and program value erodes over time, largely without notice. Treat points as currency to convert into a specific trip soon, not a store of value to hold indefinitely.

### ANA via Virgin Atlantic — the sweet spot for Japan business class

ANA's "The Room" business-class product rivals international first class on some routes — enclosed suites, not just a lie-flat seat.

| Route | Virgin Atlantic points | Competitor cost |
|---|---|---|
| West Coast → Tokyo | 52,500 | 100,000+ |
| East Coast/Europe → Tokyo | 60,000 | 100,000+ |
| Surcharges (round trip) | ~$170-200 | — |
| Cash ticket equivalent | $4,000-8,000 | — |

Booking process:

1. Search **United.com** for Star Alliance Saver availability — this surfaces ANA award space.
2. Call Virgin Atlantic (1-800-365-9500) to book the transfer.
3. Request a 48-hour hold (free) to lock the fare while you transfer points.
4. Book 330+ days out for the best availability.

### Avios ecosystem — Qatar Qsuites

Qsuites is widely regarded as the benchmark business-class product: double beds, enclosed suites, dine-on-demand service.

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Cost | 70,000-80,000 Avios one-way, business class |
| Booking | Qatar Privilege Club (361 days out; better availability visibility than booking via BA) |
| Ground experience | The Al Mourjan lounge in Doha is a strong layover experience in its own right |

## Shoulder-season windows by region

Target the roughly two-week windows that deliver most of peak-season weather and conditions with a meaningful drop in crowds.

### Mediterranean — September 15 to October 15

School holidays have ended, the sea is still swimmable, wine harvest is underway in Tuscany, and truffle season is beginning.

| Sub-destination | Notes |
|---|---|
| Greek Islands | Naxos/Paros beat Santorini for a calmer trip; 24-28°C is typical |
| Amalfi Coast / Tuscany | Dining becomes accessible without booking months ahead |
| Athens | Rain probability roughly doubles from early to late October — front-load the window |

### Southeast Asia — late October to November

| Region | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| North (Hanoi / Halong / Sapa) | Excellent | Cool, dry, clear skies — best trekking conditions of the year |
| South (Ho Chi Minh City / Mekong) | Good | Entering dry season, good for river travel |
| Central (Hoi An / Danang) | Avoid | Peak typhoon and flood months |

Also avoid late January/early February in Vietnam specifically — Tet effectively shuts the country down.

### Patagonia — March 1-20 (the "secret season")

Peak summer (December/January) brings winds up to 100 km/h that can ruin hiking days. By early-to-mid March, winds subside, autumn color emerges, and crowds drop by 40-50%. Temperatures are cooler (12-14°C) but the calm conditions make it more pleasant on the ground than a windy 20°C summer day.
