# Rotation Templates

**What this framework claims:** Hub-and-spoke (see [[trip_architecture]]) reduces decision fatigue within a single trip. A rotation calendar is the same idea applied to a full year — a small number of multi-month blocks, each anchored to one city, chosen against a fixed set of criteria instead of drifting from place to place ad hoc. The four templates below are worked examples of that calendar, not a recommendation of specific cities for you. Swap in whatever cities score well against your own criteria and keep the shape: six two-month blocks, one of them reserved for home base.

## Selection criteria

Score each candidate block against four generic dimensions:

- **Climate preference** — the season and daylight quality you want for that block (escaping your home region's worst season, chasing a specific season elsewhere, or just wanting more daylight hours in winter).
- **Sport/hobby access** — whatever your primary physical or social anchor activity is. Treat this as a hard filter: a city that fails it is eliminated regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.
- **Cost target** — a cost-of-living band you're comfortable running for two months, not a number you back into after the fact.
- **Home-base anchor** — at least one block a year reserved for admin, mail, medical care, and the place or people you consider home base, wherever that is.

Two months per block is long enough to establish gym, grocery, and social routines — see [[working_month_method]] for the same rhythm-first logic compressed into a single 30-day working trip — and short enough that a bad pick costs you two months, not a year.

## Template A — Single-region rotation (timezone-constrained)

Use this shape if your work requires you to stay in or near one timezone for most of the year — the common case is an employer or team clustered in a specific region.

| Months | Location | COL/mo | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Warm/coastal city in-region | €1,300-2,400 | Escapes home-region winter without leaving the timezone |
| Mar-Apr | Culturally dense secondary city | €1,500-3,200 | Season change, social recharge |
| May-Jun | Major city with deep infrastructure for your primary activity | €1,800-3,000 | Long days, best access to whatever your sport/hobby anchor is |
| Jul-Aug | Home base | €3,500-4,500 | Admin, address maintenance, the people you call home |
| Sep-Oct | Shoulder-season value pick | €1,200-2,200 | Good weather, low crowds, cheap |
| Nov-Dec | Indoor-season city with a strong social/holiday scene | €1,300-2,300 | Indoor infrastructure covers the season, holidays add social density |
| **Annual COL estimate** | | **~€24,000-34,000** | Staying in one region caps flight costs and keeps a single social/fitness network warm all year |

## Template B — Global rotation (flexible or async work)

Use this shape if your work tolerates async periods or doesn't require live-hours overlap with any one region — the constraint relaxes and geography opens up.

| Months | Location | COL/mo | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan-Feb | Warm, low-cost hub in a different hemisphere | $1,400-2,500 | Cheap, warm, good async work block |
| Mar-Apr | Culturally dense city in your preferred region | €2,200-3,000 | Season change, social recharge, some timezone alignment |
| May-Jun | City with peak-season access to your primary activity | $3,200-3,500 | Peak conditions for your sport/hobby anchor, friends visit |
| Jul-Aug | Home base | €3,500-4,500 | Admin, summer at home |
| Sep-Oct | Major city elsewhere in the world | $1,500-3,200 | Autumn, another deep infrastructure hub, async work block |

| **Annual COL estimate** | | **~$26,000-38,000** | Maximum variety per year — the Die With Zero (Bill Perkins) argument for front-loading varied experience over accumulating savings |

## Template C — Home-timezone remote anchor (reference math)

| Months | Location | COL/mo | Why |

## How to use this

- **This is job-agnostic scaffolding.** Overlay your own timezone and employer constraints on top of it — Templates A and C exist specifically because that constraint changes the math.
- **Pick your own hard filter.** Whatever your primary physical or social anchor activity is, treat lack of access to it as disqualifying for a block, the same way climate or cost would be.
- **Daylight/climate is a real constraint for a lot of people, not just an aesthetic preference** — weigh it seriously if it affects your mood or energy, and don't treat a string of dark-winter blocks as a minor tradeoff if it isn't one for you.
- **Long-term climate trajectory is a tiebreaker, not a primary driver**, at the timescale a rotation calendar operates on — it matters more for a permanent home-base decision than for a two-month block.
- **Nothing here is a commitment.** Test each block by living it rather than deciding from research alone — a two-month block is already the test; extend what works and cut what doesn't after one pass, not before.
