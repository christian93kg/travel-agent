# NOTICE — Quotation and Attribution Policy

This repo's `frameworks/` and `playbooks/` synthesize ideas, methods, and named
frameworks from published books, journal articles, and one published privacy
methodology. This file states the policy that governs how that material is
used, and lists every outside work it draws on.

## Policy

- **Short excerpts only, for commentary.** Where a framework or book's own
  terminology is used (Cooper's Color Code, the *Left of Bang* baseline +
  anomaly equation, Kahneman's peak-end rule), it's named and cited, not
  quoted at length.
- **Everything is attributed.** Every idea drawn from an outside work is
  credited to its author in the file that uses it, and again here.
- **Nothing is reproduced in full.** No chapter, worksheet, checklist, or
  substantial passage from any source is reproduced. Every framework in this
  repo is written in this repo's own structure and words — a distillation and
  application of the source's ideas, not a transcription of them.
- **No publisher blurbs, no unauthorized copies.** Nothing here was sourced
  from a pirated or unauthorized copy of any work, and no back-cover or
  marketing copy is reproduced.
- **Product and provider names** (GL.iNet, Mullvad, Airalo, Signal, and
  similar) are named because they're useful to a reader deciding what to buy
  or use — they're recommendations, not endorsements, and not affiliated
  with this repo.

If you're an author or rights holder and believe something here goes beyond
fair-use commentary, open an issue and it will be addressed.

## Attributions

### Bill Perkins — *Die With Zero*

Time-bucketing (sequencing demanding experiences into the life-stage window
where you can actually do them) and the memory-dividend framing (an
experience pays a return every time it's recalled, retold, or drawn on, for
the rest of your life) run through `frameworks/travel_philosophy.md`,
`frameworks/trip_architecture.md`, `frameworks/situational_awareness.md`, and
`playbooks/gear_intake_workflow.md`. Age references use the book's own
generic reference ages (30 vs. 65), never a specific traveler's age.

### Jeff Cooper — the Color Code

The White/Yellow/Orange/Red readiness-state framework, adapted from its
original combat-training context to city-street situational awareness, in
`frameworks/situational_awareness.md`.

### Patrick Van Horne & Jason A. Riley — *Left of Bang*

The baseline-and-anomaly method (developed for the Marine Corps Combat
Hunter Program) — establish what's normal, treat deviations as a decision
point — cited by name in `frameworks/situational_awareness.md`.

### Daniel Kahneman — the peak-end rule; experienced vs. remembered utility

The finding that people remember an experience by its most intense moment
and how it ended, not the moment-to-moment average, cited in
`frameworks/trip_architecture.md` §2 as the basis for engineering a trip's
peak and protecting its final day. The experienced-vs.-remembered utility
distinction (his experiencing/remembering-selves research) underpins the
memory-dividend framing in `frameworks/travel_philosophy.md`.

### Gavin de Becker — *The Gift of Fear*

The manipulation-pattern catalog (forced teaming, unsolicited help that
creates obligation, discounting a "no") referenced in
`frameworks/situational_awareness.md`'s discussion of the pre-incident
"interview" a street-crime offender runs before approaching a target.

### Marc MacYoung

The "informal interview" framing for the observation-then-approach pattern
that precedes most opportunistic street crime, cited in
`frameworks/situational_awareness.md`.

### Dr. William Dodson — the interest-based nervous system model

The framing that ADHD-pattern attention runs on novelty, urgency, challenge,
and passion rather than importance or effort, applied throughout
`frameworks/adhd_travel_design.md`.

### Michael Bazzell — *Extreme Privacy* (published methodology)

The device-hardening, alias-booking, hotel-check-in, and travel-router
methodology in `playbooks/opsec/pre_trip_opsec.md`,
`playbooks/opsec/in_transit_security.md`, and
`playbooks/opsec/communication_protocols.md` draws substantially on Bazzell's
published methodology, adapted down from his own high-threat-model use case
to what's proportionate for ordinary travel. Cited by name and edition where
a specific technique is drawn from the book directly.

### Academic research cited (findings only, not quoted)

`frameworks/situational_awareness.md` cites the following studies by name for
their findings, not by passage:

- Grayson & Stein (1981), *Journal of Communication* — the original
  victim-selection study (convicted offenders rating pedestrian footage for
  "suitability").
- Wheeler, Book & Costello (2009); Book, Costello & Camilleri (2013); Gunns,
  Johnston & Hudson (2002) — follow-up studies replicating and extending the
  Grayson & Stein finding to specific gait and movement markers.
